# What Saber Should I Buy or Bring?

This is usually everyone's first question.

Bring anything that's a genuine **stunt-combat saber** — most sabers from Ultra Sabers, VadersVault, SaberForge, Park Sabers, and similar combat-focused brands will work fine in class. What doesn't work: replica or display sabers (like Master Replicas) built as collectibles rather than combat props. A senior student inspects sabers before they're used in class, and loaners are available if you don't have one yet.

## Expect it to break

If you're doing combat choreography, treat your stunt saber as a consumable, not a keepsake. Wiring loosens, battery packs fail, bulbs blow, and blades bend — that's normal wear from repeated strikes and drops, not a sign you bought the wrong thing.

## The two-saber plan

<div class="admonition note" markdown>
<p class="admonition-title">Only budget for one? Get Saber #1.</p>
<p>You don't need two sabers to train. If cost is the concern, buy the training saber below and stop there.</p>
</div>

**Saber #1 — the training saber.** This is the one you bring to every class, leave in your trunk, and don't mind seeing scratched and dinged. Budget around **$90** at a combat-saber retailer. Pick a model sized to your height — shorter Saberists generally want a lighter, more compact hilt; taller Saberists can handle a longer one comfortably. Whatever you choose, **the blade must be rated for heavy-grade or ultra-edge heavy-grade combat use** — lighter-duty blades won't hold up to spins and grip changes.

**Saber #2 — the prize saber.** Once you're invested, this is where you spend more: a hilt you're proud to bring to conventions and photos, not something you hand your training partner to bash on. This isn't the saber you bring to a regular class or practice session.

Most Saberists do fine with a single stunt saber in the **$75–$100** range to start. Be prepared for other schools and convention environments to hit harder than SaberCraft classes do — buy with that in mind.
