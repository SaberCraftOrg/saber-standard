    Is This Class for Me? Yes!

    SaberCraft's Miami chapter started in 2015 as a small saber-based group and grew into a full ongoing class program. "Saber based" doesn't mean it begins and ends with LED sabers — there's more underneath it. Here's what a class actually covers, and why it might be for you.

    What SaberCraft classes cover

    • Dancing — learning pre-rehearsed forms with the sabers, expressing yourself in movement with a partner
    • Stage combat — not real combat. Every exchange is rehearsed for performance, the way staged fights or organized LARP combat are
    • Discipline — repeating routines until they become second nature, building focus along the way
    • Acting — practicing and improving performance and self-confidence in front of others
    • Yoga — stretching that complements the rest of your fitness routine, and better body awareness and balance
    • Meditation — quieting the noise, building gratitude, exploring the inner self
    • Imagination — room for your own creativity in costuming, photography, and expression
    • Empowerment — getting fitter and more confident, in the room and outside it
    • Community — meeting like-minded people who want saber combat partners and friends

    Who it's for

    Classes are for people who want to have fun — geeks, actors, parents, kids, or anyone chasing a sense of empowerment. It's for people who want to find the hero in themselves, who like using their imagination, and who enjoy the company of other Saberists, cosplayers, and convention regulars.

    This is not a martial arts class, and it's not fencing. It's about clashing sabers and making it look great — no helmet, no gloves, no combat gear required beyond your saber. Because nobody's getting hit in the head, it's not skewed toward any one group: SaberCraft classes run close to an even split of women and men.

    Why it's taught this way

    Swinging a saber wildly gets someone hurt. Instead, SaberCraft teaches structured dueling — specific, practiced combinations that bind you and your partner together, rather than free-for-all swinging. Most real fights end in a couple of moves; that's not what makes a scene worth watching. Staged combat is built to tell a story — the spins, the drama, the split-second hit — the same reason a duel in a film pulls you in. That's the craft this class teaches: playing out a scene that reads as a real story, told through movement, timing, and a shared system both partners understand.
